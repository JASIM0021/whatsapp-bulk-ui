Most messages a small business gets on WhatsApp are the same handful of questions: *How much is it? Are you open today? Do you deliver here? Can I book for Saturday?* An AI WhatsApp bot answers those instantly, at any hour, and leaves you the conversations that need a person.

This guide walks through setting one up without code, what to feed it so the answers are right, and the guardrails that stop it from becoming the annoying kind of bot.

## What does an AI WhatsApp bot actually do?

An AI WhatsApp bot reads each incoming message and writes a reply using information about your business. Unlike old menu bots (“press 1 for prices”), it understands free text, follows the conversation and answers in a natural way.

A useful bot does four things well:

- **Answers routine questions** from your own information, not guesses.
- **Shares the right link** at the right moment: your booking page, store or price list.
- **Knows when to stop** and passes the chat to a human.
- **Stays out of the way** when you are already replying yourself.

## What do I need before I start?

Just three things:

1. **A WhatsApp number** your customers already use. You connect it by scanning a QR code, like WhatsApp Web.
2. **Your business details**: name, a two or three sentence description and your four or five main services or products.
3. **Your links**: a booking or appointment link and a store or products link, if you have them.

## What should I feed the bot so it gives good answers?

The bot can only be as accurate as the information you give it. Write your description the way you would explain your business to a new employee:

- **Be specific.** “We deliver across Pune within 24 hours, free over ₹999” beats “fast delivery available”.
- **Include prices or price ranges** if customers ask about them. If prices vary, say so and say what they depend on.
- **List what you don’t do.** “We don’t do home visits” prevents a lot of back and forth.
- **Add your booking link** so the bot can move interested people to the next step.

Review the first few days of conversations. Every time the bot gives a weak answer, it usually means a fact is missing from your description. Add it, and the next answer improves.

## Which guardrails should I turn on?

These settings are what make customers trust the bot:

- **Human handoff by keyword.** Pick words like “human”, “call me”, “complaint” or “refund”. When a customer uses one, the chat goes to your human agent number.
- **Excluded numbers.** Family, staff and suppliers shouldn’t get bot replies. Add their numbers to the exclusion list.
- **Co-pilot mode.** Turn on “only reply when I’m offline” and set an idle time. If you answer a chat yourself, the bot stays quiet.
- **Operating hours.** Limit the bot to certain hours if you only want it covering evenings and weekends.
- **Bot detection.** Flag contacts who send an unusual number of messages per hour, which is often another bot.

## What mistakes make WhatsApp bots annoying?

- **Pretending to be human.** Let the greeting make clear it’s an assistant. People are fine with bots that are honest and helpful.
- **Trapping people.** Always offer a way to reach a person.
- **Long replies.** WhatsApp is read on phones. Keep answers to a few short lines.
- **Out-of-date information.** Update your details when prices, hours or services change.

## How do I know it’s working?

Look at how many chats the bot handled, how many it handed over and the questions that come up most. If the same question keeps reaching you, add the answer to the bot’s details.
